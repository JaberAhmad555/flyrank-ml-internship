Refresh Opportunity Scoring: Ranking Pages for Human Content Review

A leakage-aware decision-support study using anonymized Google Search data.

Abstract

This study asks whether safe, decision-time Google Search Console signals can rank content pages that are more likely to show a meaningful next-month visibility decline. I aggregate February 2026 search signals from the gated FlyRank internship warehouse and define a March decline proxy using a strict past-to-future window. A transparent hand-written baseline is compared with a Random Forest on the same client-group holdout split so no client appears in both train and test. On the same client-group holdout, Precision@50 moved from 0.220 for the baseline to 0.400 for the Random Forest (1.82x). The result is a decision-support queue with reason codes for human content review, not a claim about Google's algorithm or proof that refreshing a page causes recovery.

Introduction / Problem Statement

Content teams can have far more pages than they can manually inspect. The decision supported here is therefore not “will a refresh cause growth?” but “which pages deserve review first?” I frame the problem as ranking/scoring and use a next-month decline proxy so the output can be evaluated honestly against a future window.

Data

The analysis uses the gated FlyRank internship warehouse, specifically the public-safe pseudonymized fact_content_daily_performance release. February 2026 is the feature window and March 2026 is the outcome window; June 2026 remains sealed during development. Pages require at least 100 measured February impressions and measured GSC data in both months.

Excluded from modeling: March-derived values, raw client names, domains, URLs, titles, private queries, credentials, and any information not knowable at the February decision moment.

Methodology

Five February-only features are used: impressions, clicks, CTR, impression-weighted average position, and active search days. The proxy label is 1 when measured March impressions are at least 20% lower than measured February impressions. A transparent baseline combines visibility, low CTR, and weak position. The Random Forest is evaluated on the same 20% client-group holdout, with explicit leakage guards that reject future/label fields.

Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The label is an observed next-month decline proxy, not a causal refresh-effect label.
  • One February→March window does not establish universal behavior across seasons or clients.
  • Eligibility and GSC availability affect which pages enter the frame.
  • Feature importance describes this fitted model; it is not causal evidence.
  • The ranked output is decision support for human review, not an automated publishing decision.
  • This work does not claim to reverse-engineer Google's ranking algorithm.

Action Playbook

  • HIGH_VISIBILITY_LOW_CTR: inspect snippet/title intent match and content usefulness before editing.
  • HIGH_VISIBILITY_WEAK_POSITION: review depth, freshness, internal linking, and whether the page still matches the query need.
  • LOW_ACTIVE_DAYS: verify whether the pattern is seasonal, measurement-related, or a genuine visibility loss.
  • MULTI_SIGNAL_REVIEW: perform a human review before choosing refresh, protect, merge, or monitor.
